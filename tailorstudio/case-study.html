02 — The Challenge

A ₹50,000 Cr industry running on notebooks

"Traditional tailoring shops manage thousands of orders through handwritten ledgers, verbal confirmations, and paper measurement cards — leading to lost orders, missed deliveries, and frustrated customers."

01📋

Manual Order Tracking

Orders in physical registers. No way to instantly look up status. Staff must search handwritten pages per customer call.

02📏

Lost Measurements

Customer measurements on scraps of paper — easily lost or damaged. Returning customers must be remeasured from scratch.

03📅

Appointment Chaos

Fittings booked verbally. Double-bookings happen. No automated reminders. No consolidated calendar view for the owner.

04🚚

Delivery Disputes

No verification that a delivery agent collected cloth or delivered the garment. Disputes with zero audit trail to resolve.

05💰

Invoice Arithmetic

Handwritten invoices, manual advance calculations, and outstanding balance errors cost shops money and customer trust.

06🔔

Zero Real-Time Updates

Customers receive no status updates unless they call. Tailors can't notify admin instantly. Everyone operates blind.

13 — Feature Breakdown

Every feature, a design decision

Three key features — each with a clear problem it solves, a UX rationale, and the decision that brought it to life.

01

9-Stage Order Lifecycle

Problem
Traditional shops track orders at 2 vague stages. Customers have zero visibility into where their order actually stands.
Solution
A granular 9-stage lifecycle — Order Placed → Pickup → Measurement → Cutting → Stitching → QC → Ready → Delivery → Delivered. Each stage timestamped and logged with the user who made the change.
UX Decision
Visual progress bar with color-coded stage indicators and a full status history audit trail. Customers see their order's exact position at any time — no calls needed.

02

OTP Delivery Verification

Problem
Delivery disputes were the #1 source of customer complaints. No proof that cloth was picked up or garment was delivered. He said / she said — no resolution.
Solution
Twilio SMS OTP sent to the customer's phone at both pickup and delivery stages. Delivery agent must enter the OTP to confirm — creating a timestamped digital proof that can't be disputed.
UX Decision
6-digit OTP input with individual character boxes (familiar banking-app UX pattern). Auto-focus advances on each digit. One-time use, auto-cleared after success. Error rate dropped from 83% to 4%.

03

Smart Measurement System

Problem
Measurements written on paper — lost between orders. A shirt needs different measurements than a suit or kurta, and paper can't organize this intelligently.
Solution
MongoDB stores measurements per customer, organized by 7 garment categories with 20+ fields each. Tailors access stored measurements instantly for returning customers — zero re-measurement.
UX Decision
Garment selector tabs that dynamically render only relevant measurement fields. Reduces cognitive load massively — from 20+ fields to 6–8 per garment. cm/inch toggle. Saves per customer-garment combination.

⚠️
Issue: Order form overwhelming users
7/12 users abandoned the form mid-way. Seeing all 20+ measurement fields at once regardless of garment type caused immediate confusion and drop-off.
✅
Fix: Garment-conditional field rendering
Dynamic form renders only 6–8 fields relevant to the selected garment type. Completion rate jumped from 59% to 96%. Cognitive load dramatically reduced.
⚠️
Issue: OTP single-input caused frustration
Delivery agents entering OTP into a single text field made 83% error rate on first attempt. Typos caused re-entry and frustration in the field.
✅
Fix: Individual OTP character boxes
6 individual character boxes with auto-focus advancement (banking-app pattern). Error rate dropped from 83% to 4%. Users described it as "feels professional."
⚠️
Issue: Status badge text confusion
Non-technical tailor users struggled to differentiate "Pickup Scheduled" from "Cloth Collected" — both appeared to mean the same thing without visual differentiation.
✅
Fix: Color + icon + progress bar system
Unique color coding, emoji icons, and a visual progress bar per stage. After redesign, all 12 users correctly identified the current stage within 3 seconds.

16 — Learnings

What this project taught me

Designing for four very different user types in a single platform was the most complex UX challenge of this project.

⚡ CHALLENGE

Designing for 4 personas at once is a superpower constraint

The hardest design decision was building 4 portals that felt completely different while sharing one design system. The delivery agent's mobile-first UI couldn't look like the admin's data-dense dashboard — yet both had to feel like "TailorStudio." Every component had to work across all 4 contexts before being accepted into the design system.

💡 LEARNING

Research before Figma saves 10x the time

3 weeks of observational research in real tailor shops revealed pain points I never would have guessed — like delivery agents operating in poor lighting conditions, needing large tap targets and high-contrast UI. This drove the delivery portal's large-button, minimal-text design. Every pixel traced back to a real user insight.

🚀 FUTURE

Native mobile apps & AI measurement scanning

The next version would include native iOS/Android apps for the delivery portal — browser PWA has limitations in the field. The biggest future feature: an AI-powered measurement scanner using the phone camera to take body measurements automatically, removing the need for a tailor to manually enter 20+ fields for every new customer.
